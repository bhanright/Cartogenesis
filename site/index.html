Cartogenesis

Open source, free to use

Give it a seed. It builds the plates, the weather and the peoples.

Plates collide and erode, winds and currents decide where the rain falls, rivers find the sea, and realms take the ground between them — then it draws the map.

How a world is made

Plates and mountains

Drifting plates collide, and what they build depends on what meets: coastal ranges with volcanoes behind them, broad plateaus, island arcs, rift valleys that flood into chains of gulfs.

Erosion

Slopes collapse, rivers cut valleys and carry the debris down to floodplains, fans and deltas. Basins drain through the notches their overflow cuts.

Seas and ice

Continental shelves along the coasts; glaciers in cold mountains and scoured lake country under old ice sheets.

Climate

A warm season and a cold one, ocean currents, continental interiors, monsoons and rain shadows. Biomes follow the Köppen rules, so deserts sit where deserts sit.

Rivers and lakes

Water runs downhill to the sea or into lakes. Dry basins hold only the water their catchment can keep against evaporation, so an inland sea shrinks to a salt lake.

Realms and peoples

Borders follow watersheds, rivers and coasts. Peoples spread through country that resembles their homeland, so cultures cross borders the way real ones do.

What you get

Map views
Fifteen: the fantasy map, political and peoples layers, elevation, biomes, temperature and rainfall by season, plates, ocean currents, winds, and more.
Styles
Eleven, from an atlas with hypsometric tints to vellum, ink wash, nautical chart, pen-and-ink, Mars and a colour-blind-safe palette.
Themes
Fifteen for the window itself: light, dark, high contrast, colour-blind, and styled sets from Nautical and Baroque to Matrix and Hitchcock.
Editing
Every generated name and border can be overridden, and the overrides are kept with the world.
Saving
A save holds the finished world, so it opens instantly and never changes under you. Save files move between the browser and the desktop app.
Export
PNG or WebP up to 4096 × 4096.
Resolution
512 to 4096 cells across. The browser starts at 512; the desktop app is faster and starts at 1024.
Graphics acceleration
Optional. Erosion, the slowest part, runs on the graphics processor where the browser offers WebGPU, phones included; the desktop app renders exports there too. On a 2026 phone a 1024 world takes about twenty seconds and 2048 about a minute and a half.

Practical details

Running it in a browser

Recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. The first visit downloads about 4.5 MB and is cached after that; nothing you make is uploaded anywhere.

The optional graphics-card acceleration uses WebGPU where the browser offers it.

Sizes and files

Worlds are generated from 512 to 4096 cells across and exported as PNG or WebP at up to 4096 × 4096. WebP is about a quarter the size and softens thin rivers and borders very slightly.

A save holds the finished world rather than the recipe for it, and the same file opens in the browser and on the desktop.

The Atlas is a work in progress

Today it lists a world's realms and landmarks, lets you rename them and place labels, and stops there. A full atlas is planned for a later release, targeting 4.0: named continents, seas, bays, straits and ranges, with labels set out the way a printed map sets them. Names you give now are kept with the world.

Desktop versionWindows

Bigger worlds, more threads and no download before you start. Portable zip or installer. The installer is unsigned, so Windows shows a SmartScreen warning the first time; choose “More info”, then “Run anyway”.
